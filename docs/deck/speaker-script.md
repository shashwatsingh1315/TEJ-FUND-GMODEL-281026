# Tejas deck: speaker script

About 5 minutes in total. Each slide has a time guide, what to point at (in *italics*), and what to say.
Figures are spoken the way an Indian investor hears them (lakh and crore).

---

## Slide 0: How it's built (about 70 seconds)

*Point to column 1, the chart.*

"Before any forecast, here is where every number comes from.

We sell in four markets today: Raipur, Nagpur, Vidarbha and Chhattisgarh. Together they do ₹1.6 crore a month. ₹1.44 crore of that is tile adhesive, and ₹16 lakh is Bondure.

Every one of these markets has grown the same way. Nagpur took 8% of its tile-adhesive market in three months, and was at ₹20 lakh a month by month four. Vidarbha was at ₹38 lakh a month by month seven. A market climbs fast for about six quarters, then levels off at around 30%."

*Point to column 2, the formula.*

"We turned that pattern into one formula. Market size, times our share for how long we've been selling there, times the size of the product line. Today's tile sales at 30% share tell us what one city is worth: about ₹1.37 crore of tile adhesive a month."

*Point to column 3.*

"Then we extend it in two directions. First, more lines in the markets we already serve. Wood adhesive, uPVC and waterproofing are each sized against tile adhesive using national market data. That takes Raipur from ₹46 lakh to ₹2 crore a month.

Second, more markets. New medium cities are sized 27% smaller than the ones we're in. Metros are ten times a medium city, on a slower curve that stops at 21%."

*Point to the ribbon.*

"And it repeats for a simple reason. A new line sells through a relationship we've already won. Contractors trial, then repeat, then move their wallet to us. 84% of our revenue is repeat."

## Slide 1: What we sell (about 50 seconds)

*Point to the story strip, A → B → C.*

"Here is what that formula produces.

Four live markets today. In each one, we go from two product lines to five, so Raipur grows from ₹46 lakh to ₹2 crore a month. Then we replicate: seven new markets in year one, rising to twenty-six in year four. Hiring sets that pace, not mapping. We can map a city in under two months."

*Point to the chart.*

"The chart is revenue by product line. Wood adhesive comes in Q4, uPVC in Q6, waterproofing in Q10. By September 2030, the three new lines carry most of the growth."

*Point to the ribbon.*

"Underneath it: 80% of what we sell is made in-house. We built 16 formulations and filed 4 patents in nine months. And Bondure, our newest line, reached about 10% of sales within three months."

---

## Slide 2: What we keep (about 70 seconds)

*Point to the headline, then the three cards.*

"Margins expand for three reasons, and each one is already moving in our accounts today.

Product costs. Inbound freight fell from 8.4% of revenue to 1% in nine months. As we buy at volume and move to our own formulations, product costs go from 53% to 49%.

Selling costs. Last-mile delivery fell from 10.2% to 6.3% in four months as our stores filled up. Add one relationship manager selling five lines instead of two, and selling costs go from 27% to 21%.

Fixed costs. Between April 2025 and July 2026, revenue grew 3.75 times while fixed cost grew 1.5 times. The plan carries that forward, and fixed costs fall from 34% to 14% of revenue."

*Point to the margin bridge.*

"Add the three together: plus 4, plus 7, plus 21 points. That takes us from minus 15% to plus 16%."

*Point to the ribbon.*

"And here's what we don't cut. Influencer payouts stay at about 10%, because they bring the orders. Our best relationship managers already bring ₹38 lakh a month. And 59% of orders are paid before we dispatch."

---

## Slide 3: What we need (about 50 seconds)

*Point to the story strip loop.*

"Growth needs capital, and each round buys the next.

₹40 crore buys plants, labs and working capital. That capacity takes our ARR from ₹19 crore to ₹148 crore by round 2. Round 2 raises ₹200 crore and funds the next leg. Then the loop repeats."

*Point to the chart.*

"Four rounds: ₹40, ₹200, ₹500 and ₹1,750 crore. They're fifteen, twelve and fifteen months apart, and each one lands on a much larger revenue base."

*Point to the ribbon.*

"It's capital-efficient. Every rupee of capital builds ₹3.60 of yearly revenue. We build plants a quarter before sales need them, so capacity is never the constraint. And rounds 3 and 4 bring regional brands and plants into our network."

---

## Slide 4: What you get (about 50 seconds)

*Point to the story strip.*

"So what does an investor's money become?

₹25 crore goes in at round 1, for 8.3%. Its value rises with revenue, round after round: ₹144 crore by round 2. At a September 2030 exit it's worth ₹1,256 crore. That's 50 times, or 166% a year."

*Point to the chart, then the ribbon.*

"The chart shows that value quarter by quarter. Underneath it is the engine: a 16% margin, times 3.6 capital turns, gives a 59% return on capital, and it's positive within a year."

*Close.*

"That's the plan. We started from how our own markets actually grew. We wrote down the pattern, and we built every slide from it."

---

## Delivery notes
- On slide 0, slow down. If the VC understands the formula, every later slide reads as a consequence of it.
- On slides 3 and 4, let the numbers on screen do the work. Don't add valuation or pricing detail beyond what's spoken here.
- If asked about a figure in a headline, answer from the slide body. The body figures (₹25 Cr, ₹148 Cr ARR, 13% a month) are the model's own.
